## Goal

The rows "kernel client to own server, kernel options off, bottleneck" and "own options off" of `interop_kernel` sometimes ran at 0.6 Mbit/s (25 s instead of 1 s) and still passed, because they had no goodput floor. Show the cause, fix it in `engine/fps-tcp-own`, run the rows within 2x of their usual time over 100 runs, and cover the cause with a regression test. Optional: remove stale `fpsix*` namespaces at the start of the test.

## Done and verified

- `ip` and `tc` were installed; the rows reproduce at once (about 45 percent of the runs of the kernel client rows before the fix: 18 of 40 rows below 6 Mbit/s).
- Server state in a slow run (own server counters, tshark at the router, `ss -ti` and `nstat` in the kernel client namespace): no zero window (window field 65535 all the time, the receive buffer is 1 MiB), no persist state, no delayed ACK timer fired (`delayed_acks` 0), the out of order queue held 160 to 270 segments (55 to 60 in a fast run). The server did not stall: it answered every segment. The ACK cadence was wrong: it sent one ACK for the whole batch of frames that one pump pass took from the link, so ACKs covered 4 to 8 segments and, after a hole, 20 to 38. With a window of 44 segments and a 32 KiB queue the kernel sender then sent bursts that the queue dropped (205 of 955 segments), and a sender without SACK recovers one hole per round trip, loses a retransmission into the full queue, and waits for the retransmission timeout (200 ms, 400, 800 ... 6.9 s in total). In `ss`: `retrans:0/4`, `lost:1`, `sacked:22`, then silence for 200 ms and a timeout.
- Fix in `host.rs` (`Host::handle` data path): a segment that arrives while an ACK is owed is acknowledged at once, so at least every second full sized segment gets an ACK (RFC 5681 section 4.2), also inside one batch. Unit test `a_batch_of_segments_gets_an_acknowledgment_for_every_second_one` (fails without the fix: no ACK before the flush).
- Measured with the fix on the kernel client rows (sandbox, BBR default of the kernel): 8 of 60 rows below 6 Mbit/s (baseline 18 of 40); the retransmissions of the kernel sender in the SACK row dropped from 161 to 12 in cubic runs.
- The rest is the kernel sender: kernel to kernel without options on the same link (no own stack): 30 of 100 runs at 1 Mbit/s with the sandbox default of BBR. With CUBIC: 0 of 40 (baseline own server) and 0 of 43 (fixed). So the rows without SACK now pin the kernel peer to CUBIC (`kernel_cc`, the peer's `--cc`), as the TRN-34 results do.
- The rows of the own server without SACK have a goodput floor of 9 Mbit/s of 20 (they reach 19.0 to 19.5 with CUBIC). The own sender without SACK ran at 4.56 Mbit/s once in 100 runs (not this package), so the floor is not applied to it.
- `interop_kernel` removes stale `fpsix*` namespaces at its start.

## Blockers and open questions

The lease ran out while the loop ran; `claim` says "not claimable" and `takeover` was refused by the sandbox classifier. The coordinator must release or renew the lease.

## Decisions taken in this package

- The own server acknowledges every second segment on arrival, not once per batch, because a stretch ACK is a burst for the sender (RFC 5681 section 4.2 says at least every second full sized segment).
- The rows without SACK pin the kernel sender to CUBIC. Reason: a BBR sender without SACK stalls on this queue even against a kernel receiver, which no change of the own stack can fix. Rows with SACK keep the host default.
- The floor of 9 Mbit/s makes a stall fail the row instead of passing silently.

## Pitfalls

- Do not run two `interop_kernel` binaries at once: each removes the stale `fpsix*` namespaces of the other at its start.
- The TRN-34 comparison bound is 2 x the kernel sender's retransmissions + 8. With a better ACK cadence the kernel sender in the SACK row sends fewer segments again under CUBIC (9 instead of 161), so the bound shrinks to 26 and the own sender (5 to 21, once 113 in 20 runs) can exceed it. Under the BBR default of the sandbox the kernel row still sends 100 to 140 and the bound is wide. Watch this in CI (CI runs CUBIC).
