## [2026-11-12] ingest | MOD-23 found why the http2 capture counted 39 of 40 TLS sessions

MOD-23 looked at the failure of job 130722417029 (run 43077861098, pull request #473), where `realism decrypted` of the `module (http2)` job printed "the capture has 39 TLS sessions, expected 40" and the rerun passed.

What the log shows. The same capture scored 40 of 40 in the conformance check (`TLS: 40`), 40 of 40 sampled sessions decrypted in the encryption check, and 40 with the JA4 of their profile. Every ClientHello was in the capture, and the generator had opened 40 connections to the ports 8443 and 8444. Only the decrypted check counted 39. That check lists the TLS sessions as the streams with a ClientHello that a plain `tshark -Y "tls.handshake.type == 1"` finds, and tshark finds one on a port that is not a TLS port by its heuristic only. The encryption check passes `-d tcp.port==P,tls` and does not depend on it.

What we reproduced. Cutting the first ClientHello of the committed capture after 1 to 4 octets gives the exact message of the failed run (39 TLS sessions, expected 40, with 39 of 39 decrypting), and a cut after 5 octets or more gives 40. Reordering, duplicating, or dropping the SYN, SYN-ACK, ACK, or ClientHello frames of a session leaves the count at 40. A reused client source port, which 2 of 11 captures of 100 sessions per scenario had (6 reused 5-tuples in 2200 TLS connections), does not give 39: tshark keeps two streams, and the realism runner joins the two connections into one session, which would have printed 39 of 39 in the conformance row, and the failed run printed 40 of 40. So the capture of the failed run held 40 distinct connections and a ClientHello that tshark did not take for TLS. We could not read that capture, because the artifact download is refused in the sandbox (the blob host answers 403 to the proxy), so what split or changed that ClientHello is not known. The eleven captures, recorded in the namespace harness with 200 TLS connections each and most of them under two busy loops on two CPUs, never showed a ClientHello that tshark missed.

Fix, inside `modules/http2/`: `fixtures/capture.sh` runs the decrypted check with `--tshark fixtures/tshark-tls-ports.sh`, which adds `-d tcp.port==P,tls` for the https ports when tshark reads the capture and runs tshark unchanged for every other file, because the decrypted view keeps those ports with HTTP/2 in the clear. When the check fails, `capture.sh` prints the SYNs, the distinct 5-tuples, and the ClientHello streams of the https ports. `tests/tshark_wrapper.rs` checks the wrapper with a stand-in tshark and, with real tshark, that a capture with a 1 octet first segment of its first ClientHello lists 40 streams through it (39 without).
