## [2026-11-13] ingest | MOD-31 HLS timing and bytes as labels

The hls fixture now checks its segment cadence and its bytes through the `temporal` labels of `modules/hls/fixtures/expected.yaml`, which RLM-22 made possible, and `hls_check.py`, `test_hls_check.py`, and `hls.playbacks.json` are gone, as `rtp_check.py` went in MOD-24. The `capture.sh` script, `mk/mod-hls.mk`, and `.github/workflows/mod-hls.yml` no longer call the script. The labels are the block that the RLM-22 log entry gave, and they keep the `arrivals` label of `hls_segment`:

- `temporal.packets` for `hls_vod`, `hls_live`, and `hls_segment`: client direction, `path: '/v\d+/seg\d+\.ts$'`, a constant 400 ms with `tolerance_ms: 40`, `within: 0.9`, `mean_tolerance_ms: 20`, and `min_samples: 3`. `hls_segment` has `join_ms: 800`, which makes the segment connections of `vod_segments` one playback.
- `temporal.rungs` for the same three applications: the ladder `160000, 320000, 640000` bit/s, 400 ms per segment, `min_samples: 4`, `walk: up` for the two vod applications, and `streams` of 3, 1, and 3. The tolerances are the defaults of the labels (188 octets for a body, 5% for the byte share and the bit rate).

The strength is that of the script. A mean gap within 5% of the segment duration is `mean_tolerance_ms: 20`, 90% of the gaps within 10% is `tolerance_ms: 40` with `within: 0.9`, a body within one transport stream packet is `body_tolerance_bytes`, and the byte share per rung and the bit rate within 5% are `share_tolerance` and `rate_tolerance`. The vod playbacks walk up the whole ladder and the live playbacks do not have to, as before. Three differences from the script remain, and the first two were the findings of RLM-22: a playback with fewer than `min_samples` requests is counted as too short and not failed, so the `streams` count is what catches a missing playback; the playbacks are counted per application (3, 1, 3) and not as one number for the capture (7); and the labels join segment connections that are less than `join_ms` (two segment durations) apart, as the script did with its own constant.

Evidence is `modules/hls/fixtures/test_temporal_labels.py` (23 tests, `python3 -m unittest discover -s modules/hls/fixtures`, pure Python, no tshark and no nDPI, which the mod-hls workflow already runs):

- The committed capture passes with the labels of the file. Its 7 playbacks are judged (3, 3, and 1 per application, 32 gaps in the one playback of segment connections), and the bit rate of each is within 1% of the ladder (375327 against 373333 bit/s for each ladder playback, 0.5%).
- The same capture with the time inside every playback stretched by 1.15 or 0.8 fails for all three applications. A stretch of 1.06 (every gap inside 40 ms, the mean 24 ms off) fails only through `mean_tolerance_ms`, and it passes when that label is taken out. `python3 -m realism score` with nDPI 4.2 and tshark 4.2.2 exits 1 for the 1.15 and the 1.06 copies and 0 for the committed capture (the `hls.report.json` is scored again, report format 0.20.0, `temporal` passes).
- Synthetic playbacks fail for the wrong bit rate: a ladder whose top rung is 20% short (a body of 25568 octets against 32000), a live rung 25% long, and a segment connection whose rung 0 is twice as large. They also fail for a drifting cadence (432 ms, inside the tolerance, caught by the mean), a burst, a missing playback (2 of the 3 vod playbacks), and a vod playback that stays on rung 0. The same writer with the right bytes and the right cadence passes.
- `LabelsMatchParamsTest` reads `params.json` and compares it with the labels: the ladder, the segment duration (also the packet time and the arrival time), `join_ms` as two segment durations, the playback counts, the walk for the vod scenarios only, the number of stages with the number of rungs, the minimum sample counts against the length of each playback, the application of each port, and the rung path with the segment path.

The report `modules/hls/fixtures/hls.report.json` was scored again (it was at format 0.11.0), so that it holds the packet gap and rung comparisons. The README of the module and the module and Modules pages describe the labels in place of the script.

Not verified here: the CI run of the mod-hls job, which installs the pinned nDPI 4.14 and tshark (this sandbox has nDPI 4.2.0 and tshark 4.2.2), and `capture.sh`, which needs root and the namespace harness (`bash -n` passes). Zeek is not part of the hls job.
