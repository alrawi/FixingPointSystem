## [2026-11-12] ingest | QA-11 The parser findings of the Gate 2 mix stage

A failed parser error check of Gate 2 now names each finding in the job log. The report of `gate2_report.py` has a `Parser findings` section for the mix (and one for each module with findings), and the mix stage runs the new `mix_findings.py`, which prints a line for each finding with its place in the capture, the facts of its session, and the two causes that a finding without a protocol fault has had so far (a capture boundary, `cut`, and two connections of one 5-tuple, `reused_tuple`). `mix.sh` prints the packet count and the first and last packet time of the capture, and `mix/parser-findings.json` keeps the same facts. `prove-failure.sh` has a fourth proof, and its module proof now checks that the report names the finding. The evidence is `docs/handoff/QA-11.md`, `tests/gates/gate2/test_mix_findings.py`, and `test_gate2_report.py` (63 tests in the suite).

Cause of the findings: the first run of this branch that failed (run 43233842055, attempt 3) had 31 tshark findings and none from Zeek. The log named them: one `pcp` malformed packet in an `http2` session on TCP 44322, and 30 `manolito` malformed packets in an RTP stream (`aux`) on UDP 41170. The kernel picks the client port of a session at random, and tshark 4.2.2 binds 922 TCP and UDP ports to a dissector, among them TCP 44322 (`pmproxy`) and UDP 41170 (`manolito`), so tshark decoded the payload with a dissector that does not fit it. This is the likely cause of the 10 findings of PR 486 too, whose artifacts the sandbox could not read. The named rule is the port collision rule: a tshark finding does not count when the client port of its session is bound to a dissector for the transport and the server port is not bound to the same dissector. It needs a `parser-findings.json` that lists every finding of the realism report, never applies to Zeek, and leaves the finding in the log and the report marked `not counted`. We described it in [Gates](Gates.md#parser-findings-and-the-port-collision-rule) and in the README of the suite.

Findings:

- The two reports of PR 486 after the first failure were not two failures of the mix. Job logs can be read with `gh api repos/OWNER/REPO/actions/jobs/ID/logs` even where artifacts cannot. Attempt 1 failed the mix job with 10 findings. The mix job of attempt 2 passed with 0 findings (client run `run_fff8389d...`), and the report jobs of attempts 2 and 3 judged the artifact of attempt 1 (client run `run_a48cc9ec...`), because a rerun of failed jobs leaves two artifacts named `gate2-mix` and `actions/download-artifact` takes one of them by an id order that does not follow time (the older artifact had the higher id). Attempt 4 passed because its jobs all ran again and the new artifact was the one that the download took. This is a defect of `.github/workflows/qa-gate2.yml`, outside the paths of QA-11, so the coordinator applies it (see the Shared page notes).
- The port collision rule is in the suite for now, as QA-09 put the reuse rule there before RLM-17 moved it into `ZEEK_STATE_WEIRDS`. The better place is the realism runner: a follow up in the RLM workstream can make `TsharkRunner` or the parser error check leave out the findings of a session whose client port is bound to a dissector, and then the suite drops the rule.
- The module stage has no such rule. Its captures hold 16 to 200 sessions, so a client port that collides is unlikely, and a collision there would still fail the gate and name the finding.
- I did not narrow the port range of the namespace (`net.ipv4.ip_local_port_range`) to ports without a dissector: 39 ports at 32768 or above are bound, the free interval above 60001 holds only 5,534 ports, and the smaller range would raise the reuse of 5-tuples that QA-09 saw.
- Zeek is not installed in the sandbox. The mix stage, `prove-failure.sh --only mix`, and the three runs in a row ran in CI only. The unit tests, `ruff`, `mypy --strict`, `shellcheck`, `prove-failure.sh --only report` on fixture results, and the `mix_findings.py` smoke test on `modules/ftp/fixtures/ftp.pcapng` (with the real `tshark -G decodes`) ran locally.
