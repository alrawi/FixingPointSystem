## [2026-11-17] ingest | RLM-24 A reused TCP client port starts a new session

The session table of the realism runner no longer merges two TCP connections that reuse a client port (issue #578). `realism/capture.py` started a session for each 5-tuple and so kept the second connection in the first one. It now starts a new session on a SYN without ACK when the connection of the key has seen a FIN or an RST in either direction, and keeps a SYN that repeats on a connection that has not closed in its session, as tshark does for `tcp.stream`. The decrypted view of `realism/decrypted.py` gives session number n the initial sequence numbers of `initial_sequence_numbers(n)`, where it gave every session 1000 and 5000, so a dissector reads a second connection and not a retransmitted SYN. The batch 35 http2 view of the harness and Gate 2 modules jobs counted 14 HTTP/2 messages in a session that held 7 for this reason.

Design choices:

- The rule reads the packets of a key and not the sequence numbers, so it stays the same for the raw capture and the view, and for the synthetic captures of the tests, which give every session the initial sequence numbers 1000 and 5000. tshark also compares sequence numbers, so a capture that reuses the key and the number is two sessions here and one stream in tshark. No capture of the repository does.
- `Session.connection` (0 for the first session of a key) and a new `SessionResolver` carry the rule to the readers that walk the frames again after `summarize`: `content.read_flows`, `exchanges.read_exchanges`, `structure.connections`, and `temporal._read_streams`. They looked a session up by its key, which is no longer unique, so the packets of both connections would have gone to the last one. The resolver replays the rule and returns None for a connection that is not among the sessions it was given, so `read_exchanges` still works on a subset of sessions.
- UDP is unchanged, because it has no FIN, RST, or SYN.
- The initial sequence numbers step by odd constants (0x9E3779B1 and 0x85EBCA6B), so no two views indexes up to 2**32 share one, and `_frame` wraps the sequence numbers at 2**32.

Evidence: `realism/tests/test_capture.py` and `realism/tests/test_decrypted.py` have new cases on synthetic captures. Two connections on one client port give two sessions (ids 1 and 2, `connection` 0 and 1, 10 packets each), a SYN after an RST gives two, a retransmitted SYN and SYN-ACK stay one session, traffic after a FIN without a new SYN stays one, and UDP on a reused port stays one. The resolver gives the packets of each connection to its session, also when only the second session is given, `read_exchanges` returns `/first` for session 1 and `/second` for session 2, and `connections` gives each its own start. The view of two sessions on one client port summarizes as two sessions with different SYN sequence numbers, and tshark numbers two `tcp.stream` values in it (the test skips without tshark). The initial sequence numbers of 400 view positions are distinct. Every committed capture (15 module fixtures and `realism/tests/fixtures/mix/mix.pcapng`) has the same session table before and after the change: same ids, endpoints, packet counts, and bytes, so no fixture changed its result. `make lint-rlm test-rlm` passes.

Findings:

- Results keyed by the 5-tuple still apply to every session of the key: the nDPI verdict, the tshark findings of the parser error check, the tshark message counts and the TLS hello and Finished records. nDPI itself keeps one flow per 5-tuple. A capture with a reused port therefore counts the session twice in the table and attributes a parser finding to the last session of the key. This is not a regression, since both sessions were one before, and per connection attribution of tshark output would need `tcp.stream` in every field list.
- No fixture reuses a client port, so no committed fixture shows the fix. The proof is the synthetic captures and the view test with tshark.

Shared page notes:

- [Module Development](Module-Development.md), section "Watch out for", subsection "Listeners, ports, and spawned flows". Replace the last sentence of the entry "The kernel reuses a client source port." (the one that starts "Give every session of a fixture peer its own source port") with the sentence below, which keeps the advice and adds what the realism runner does now:

  > Give every session of a fixture peer its own source port, make `capture.sh` fail with the cause when the capture holds fewer sessions than ran, and key connections in tests by the four tuple, never by source host and port alone (ENG-28). The realism runner splits a reused port the way tshark does: a SYN without ACK on a four tuple whose connection has seen a FIN or an RST starts a new session, and a repeated SYN of an open connection stays in its session, so a fixture that reuses a port still counts every connection (RLM-24). The tshark findings and the nDPI verdict of such a four tuple are one result for both sessions, so a parser error or a mislabel cannot be tied to one of them, and the way to a clean per session result is still a source port of its own.

- [Module Development](Module-Development.md), section "Watch out for", add this entry after the one above:

  > **The decrypted view of two sessions on one client port counts the messages twice.** You see a message count of 14 in a session that should hold 7 in the `messages` check of a decrypted view, while the capture has two connections. The view keeps the client port of every session, and a view that gives every session the same initial sequence numbers is one connection with a SYN that repeated, so a dissector joins the sessions. `python3 -m realism decrypted` now gives each session of the view its own initial sequence numbers (`realism.decrypted.initial_sequence_numbers`), and the session table starts a new session on a SYN after a FIN or an RST. If you write a view or a capture by hand, give each connection its own initial sequence number, and close a connection with a FIN or an RST before the next one starts on the same ports (RLM-24, issue #578).
