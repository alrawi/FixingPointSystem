## [2026-11-12] ingest | MOD-25 byte estimates in every module manifest

MOD-25 gives every scenario of the 12 first party modules that had none an `expected_bytes` entry measured from the module tests, corrects the four hand written `sip_rtp` estimates, and adds a guard test `default_parameters_move_the_expected_bytes` to each of the 14 modules with scenarios (`hls` had one). The guard runs every scenario of the manifest with its default parameters, adds the payload bytes of both directions of every connection, and fails at a difference of 2 percent or more. It also fails for a key of `expected_bytes` that names no scenario, which the validator does not check. `FPS_GATE2_BASIS` leaves `.github/workflows/qa-gate2.yml`, so the suite runs the bytes basis with no waiver for the basis.

What was measured:
- Scenarios whose sizes come from the session generator vary a lot between sessions (`ftp/retr_epsv` between 46000 and 413000 bytes, the mail scenarios between 36000 and 650000). Their guard averages over `ESTIMATE_SESSIONS` session ids under the seed 42 (100 for `ftp`, `smtp`, `imap`, `pop3`, 40 for `ssh`, 20 for the others, 10 for `smb2`), so the estimate is reproducible. A mean over 20 sessions moved by up to 10 percent against the mean over 100, so the estimate of the random scenarios still has an error of a few percent, and the calibration of the run corrects it.
- TLS scenarios count the plaintext above the TLS layer (`Run::c2s` and `Run::s2c`), and the plaintext before STARTTLS counts. UDP flows count datagram payloads. Spawned flows count through `net.conns()` (the FTP data connection, the RTP media).
- The values are rounded to three significant digits from 1000 bytes up.
- `sip_rtp` had hand written values 12 to 21 percent above the measurement for `call_pcma` (175000, measured 154030), `call_g729` (50000, 41276), `call_opus` (150000, 133646), and `busy` (1800, 1574). `call_pcmu` was right.
- `modules/_template/` has 1280, which is exactly 256 plus 1024. `content` has no scenarios, `tls` has no manifest, and `strike` is MOD-27's.

[Module Development](../Module-Development.md) now says in "How to measure it" that a scenario with drawn sizes needs the mean over many session ids, what counts as payload for TLS, datagrams, and spawned flows, how the first party manifests round, and that the guard also checks the keys. [Modules](../Modules.md) has a section "Byte estimates".

Findings for the coordinator:
- The Gate 2 suite stages its module trees with the estimates of its own `mix.yaml` for the scenarios of the mix (`mix_config.py`, `stage_modules`), and those estimates assume the mix parameters (for example `body_size` 20000). The manifest values of MOD-25 are the default parameter values, so the two sets differ on purpose. Once the bytes basis passes, the `mix.yaml` column `expected_bytes` and the stage step could be replaced by the manifests, which needs the mix to run on default sizes or a per entry override, and is a QA decision.
- `mix.yaml` says the run report seems to count the FTP data connection and the SIP media stream as sessions of their own. The manifest values count them with their scenario session, as the schema defines. If the controller calibration divides the bytes by those counted sessions, `ftp` and `sip_rtp` estimates there would look about twice too high. Nobody settled this, and the first qa-gate2 run on the bytes basis shows whether it matters.
- Not verified locally: qa-gate2 (it needs root, namespaces, and Zeek). The guard tests of the 14 crates ran here with `cargo test --offline -p <crate> --test scenarios default_parameters`. The disk of the sandbox was full, so the build used a tmpfs target directory.
